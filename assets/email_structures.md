## WHAT ACTUALLY WORKED (evidence-based)

### Emails that got 🟢 replies: Rong, Wang, Gumel, Rohr, Mordecai
Common traits:
- Short (250–350 words)
- Opened with the professor's research, not Soumitro's achievements
- Honest about gaps — did not oversell
- Asked a clear, low-friction question at the end
- Did NOT use the "structural bottleneck / this specific limitation draws me directly to your work" template

### Emails that backfired: Shiu, Pilyugin, Martcheva
Reason: cited papers that didn't actually contain the claimed methodology. Correction emails had to be sent.
**Rule: Never cite a paper for a methodology you didn't verify is in it.**

## FORBIDDEN PHRASES (zero tolerance)
- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "I am writing to express my strong interest"
- "Delve", "tapestry", "keen interest", "I would be honored"
- "structural bottleneck... this specific limitation is what draws me directly to your work" ← FLAGGED BY MULTIPLE PROFESSORS
- Any claim about a paper you haven't verified in this session

## PARAGRAPH STRUCTURE (S2 — primary template)

### Subject line
`Soumitro Kumar Das – PhD Inquiry: [specific paper topic or advertised project name]`

### Paragraph 1 — Hook on THEIR work (2-3 sentences)
Reference ONE paper. Name a specific method or finding. Connect it to an open problem.
Do NOT start with "I am writing to..."

Example that worked (Wang):
> "I was especially interested in your publication in Nonlinear Analysis: Hybrid Systems on epidemic dynamics of cholera with impulsive sanitation — specifically how pulsed intervention strategies shift the disease-free equilibrium in ways continuous ODE models cannot capture. That gap is exactly what my own modelling work has run into."

### Paragraph 2 — His specific skills → their specific need (3-4 sentences)
Name the technique, paper, numerical method. Be concrete, not general.
Draw from PROPOSAL_BANK.md items 1–6 (adapt, don't copy).
Include the dengue paper DOI and GitHub link if relevant.

Core facts to use:
- Published Q1 paper: DOI https://doi.org/10.1080/25765299.2026.2651569
- GitHub: https://github.com/SoumitroDas/dengue-carrying-capacity-model-simulations
- Fractional-order SARS-CoV-2 paper (under review, Q1) — Caputo derivative, Adams-Bashforth-Moulton method
- ML collaboration: student mental health study (under review, Q1)
- Software: MATLAB, Python (NumPy, SciPy, Scikit-learn)

Gap-statement template (from PROPOSAL_BANK item 8):
> "I have not yet worked on [topic], but I am keen to move from infectious-disease dynamics to [new area], and my modelling, statistical, and coding skills would let me contribute from the start while I learn the [domain] side from the team."

### Paragraph 3 — The ask (2 sentences max)
Simple. Low-friction. No flattery.

Template (from Rong/Wang successes):
> "I have attached my CV. I would be grateful to know whether you are currently considering PhD applicants with a background in mathematical modelling, and whether there might be a role for someone with my background in your group."

Or (for advertised positions):
> "I have attached my CV for the [position name]. If you have a moment, I would welcome any guidance on whether my background in [X] would be a suitable fit for the advertised project."

- Never send a third follow-up.

## TONE CALIBRATION
- Write as a peer-to-peer academic, not as a supplicant
- Confident but not arrogant
- Specific and curious, not gushing
- Short sentences over long ones
- Active voice throughout
- Honest about gaps — gaps + enthusiasm beats fabricated expertise every time
